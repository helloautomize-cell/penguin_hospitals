**H1: Caesarean delivery (C-section)**

A caesarean is an operation to deliver the baby through a cut in the tummy and womb. Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande recommends one when it is the safer way for mother or baby, either planned in advance or as an emergency during labour. Our operation theatre is in the same building as the labour room and is ready day and night.

### When a caesarean may be advised
**Planned:**
- The baby is lying bottom first (breech) or sideways
- Placenta previa, where the placenta covers the cervix
- Two or more previous caesareans
- Some twin pregnancies
- Certain health conditions in the mother

**During labour (emergency):**
- The baby's heartbeat shows signs of stress
- Labour stops progressing
- Heavy bleeding
- The cord comes down before the baby

### What happens
1. **Before.** You meet the anaesthetist, sign a consent form, and stop eating as advised.
2. **Anaesthesia.** Usually a spinal or epidural, so you are awake but feel no pain from the chest down. General anaesthesia is used only when needed.
3. **The operation.** A cut is made low across the tummy, usually along the bikini line. The baby is born within about 10 minutes, and the whole operation takes about 45 to 60 minutes.
4. **Meeting your baby.** Your baby is checked on the newborn warming station and brought to you as soon as possible.

### Recovery
- You can sip water within hours, and walk with help the next day.
- Most mothers go home after 3 to 4 days.
- The wound heals over about 6 weeks. Avoid heavy lifting and driving until Dr. Lakshmi says it is fine.
- Breastfeeding can start soon after birth. Pillows and side-lying positions help.

### FAQs
1. **Can I choose a caesarean without a medical reason?** It is your right to ask and to be heard. Dr. Lakshmi will discuss your reasons and fears, and explain the benefits and risks of both ways of birth. For most healthy pregnancies, a normal birth is safer, with faster recovery, and she will support you to make an informed choice.
2. **How long before I can try for another baby?** Waiting at least 18 months between a caesarean and the next birth gives the scar time to heal fully. Use contraception in the meantime, and discuss your plans with Dr. Lakshmi at your postnatal check.
3. **Will I always need a caesarean in future pregnancies?** No. After one caesarean, many women can try for a normal birth next time. After two or more, a planned caesarean is usually advised. The decision depends on why you had the caesarean and how this pregnancy is going.
4. **When can I lift my older child or do housework?** Light activities such as walking and gentle housework can start in the first weeks. Avoid lifting anything heavier than your baby for about 6 weeks. If you have older children, sit down and let them climb onto your lap instead of lifting them.
5. **What should I watch for at home?** Come back if the wound becomes red, hot, swollen or starts leaking, if you get a fever, if bleeding becomes heavy, or if one leg becomes painful and swollen. Sudden breathlessness or chest pain needs a call to 108 or 112.

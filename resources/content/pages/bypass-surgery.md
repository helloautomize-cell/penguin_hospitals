**H1: Bypass surgery (CABG)**

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) creates new routes for blood to reach the heart muscle, going around narrowed or blocked coronary arteries. The new routes are made with blood vessels taken from your chest, arm or leg. Dr. Ashish Baviskar performs bypass surgery both on the heart-lung machine and on a beating heart, and discusses with you whether bypass, stenting or medicines is the right choice.

### When bypass surgery is advised
- Blockages in several heart arteries
- A severe blockage in the left main coronary artery
- Blockages with diabetes, where bypass often works better than stents
- A weak heart pump together with blockages
- When stents are not possible or have not worked
- Sometimes as an emergency after a heart attack

### Types
- **On-pump CABG:** the heart is stopped and the heart-lung machine takes over
- **Off-pump (beating heart) CABG:** surgery on the heart while it is still beating
- **Total arterial bypass:** using arteries instead of leg veins, for grafts that may last longer
- **MICS CABG:** for selected patients, through a small cut between the ribs

### What to expect
1. **Before.** Angiography, echo, blood tests and a talk with the surgeon and anaesthetist.
2. **The operation.** Usually 3 to 5 hours under general anaesthesia.
3. **ICU.** One to two days, with close monitoring.
4. **Ward and discharge.** Walking with help in the first days. Most patients go home in about a week.
5. **At home.** Wound care, medicines, a walking plan, and no heavy lifting or driving for about 6 to 8 weeks.

### Living well after bypass
A bypass treats the blockages but not the disease that caused them. Stopping smoking, controlling sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol, regular walking, and taking your medicines keep the grafts open for longer.

### FAQs
1. **Bypass or stent: which is better?** It depends on how many arteries are blocked, where, and on your other conditions, especially diabetes and heart pump strength. For blockages in several arteries, bypass often gives better long-term results. For a single blockage, a stent may be enough. A heart team, cardiologist and surgeon, should decide with you.
2. **How long does a bypass last?** Arterial grafts often stay open for 15 to 20 years or more. Vein grafts tend to last a little less. Not smoking and controlling cholesterol, sugar and blood pressure make a big difference to how long grafts last.
3. **When can I go back to normal life?** Most people walk around the house in the first week, return to desk work in 6 to 8 weeks, and resume most activities, including intimacy, by 2 to 3 months. Driving usually waits about 6 weeks, until the breastbone has healed.
4. **Is bypass surgery risky?** All heart surgery carries risks, including bleeding, infection, irregular heartbeat, stroke and kidney problems. For most planned bypass operations, the risk is low, but it is higher in older people and those with other illnesses. Dr. Ashish will explain your personal risk before you decide.

**H1: Fertility evaluation**

If you have been trying for a baby for a year without success, or six months if you are over 35, a fertility check-up can find out why and what can help. The tests involve both partners. At Penguin Hospital, Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande starts with simple tests, tracks ovulation with scans, and explains every option, including when to go to an IVF centre.

### When to come
- After 12 months of trying, if you are under 35
- After 6 months, if you are 35 or older
- Sooner if you have irregular periods, PCOS, endometriosis, past pelvic infection or surgery, two or more miscarriages, or if your husband has had a known problem

### The first tests
**For the woman**
- A talk about cycles, past pregnancies and health
- An ultrasound of the womb and ovaries
- Blood tests for hormones, thyroid and ovarian reserve (AMH)
- A test of the tubes (HSG or a saline scan), when needed

**For the man**
- A semen analysis, which is simple and often the most useful single test
- A physician or urologist review if needed

### What can help
- **Timing.** Knowing your fertile days.
- **Ovulation tracking.** Serial scans to see when the egg is released.
- **Medicines to help ovulation**, with scan monitoring.
- **IUI (intrauterine insemination).** Washed sperm is placed in the womb at the time of ovulation. [CONFIRM: IUI done in-house or consultation only]
- **Surgery** for fibroids, polyps, endometriosis or blocked tubes, when it helps.
- **Referral for IVF** when it is the better route, with your reports ready.

### FAQs
1. **Is infertility usually the woman's problem?** No. In about a third of couples the cause is in the woman, in about a third it is in the man, and in the rest it is in both or unexplained. That is why both partners are tested from the start. A semen test is quick and easy.
2. **What are my fertile days?** In a 28-day cycle, ovulation usually happens around day 14, and the fertile window is the five days before and the day of ovulation. Sex every two to three days through the cycle covers this window without stress. Cycles vary, so scans can pinpoint ovulation.
3. **Does PCOS mean I need IVF?** Usually not. Most women with PCOS who are not ovulating can be helped with weight management and medicines to trigger ovulation, with scan monitoring. IVF is considered if these do not work or there are other factors.
4. **How many IUI cycles should we try?** Many couples try three to four cycles of IUI if the tubes are open and the semen test is suitable. If it does not work, IVF may give a better chance. Dr. Lakshmi will review your situation after each cycle.

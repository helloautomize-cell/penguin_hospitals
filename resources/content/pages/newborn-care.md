**H1: Newborn care**

The first hours after birth matter. At Penguin Hospital, every baby is received onto a newborn warming station in the labour room, checked straight away, and kept close to their mother. [CONFIRM: paediatrician present at birth, in-house or on call] We help with feeding, explain the first vaccines and screening, and tell you what to watch for at home.

### At birth
- Drying, warmth and a first check of breathing, colour and heartbeat
- Skin-to-skin with the mother as soon as possible
- Breastfeeding started within the first hour, when possible
- Weight, length and head size measured
- Vitamin K given to prevent bleeding, with your consent

### Before you go home
- A full examination of your baby by a doctor [CONFIRM paediatrician]
- Birth vaccines as per the national schedule [CONFIRM given here]
- Hearing and other newborn screening, where available [CONFIRM]
- Advice on feeding, sleeping on the back, cord care, bathing and jaundice
- Your baby's birth certificate process explained [CONFIRM]

### If your baby needs special care
Some babies need extra help with breathing, feeding, jaundice or warmth. [CONFIRM: NICU in-house, or transfer arrangement with a named hospital.] When a baby is expected to need intensive care, we plan the birth with this in mind.

**When to get help straight away.** Your baby has difficulty breathing or turns blue, has a fit, is very floppy or hard to wake, has a fever (or feels cold), stops feeding, has yellow skin in the first 24 hours or yellow palms and soles, or has blood in the stool or vomit. Call 108 or 112, or come straight in.

### FAQs
1. **Is jaundice in newborns normal?** Mild yellowing after the first day is common and usually fades within two weeks. Jaundice in the first 24 hours, spreading to the palms and soles, or a baby who is sleepy and feeding poorly needs a check the same day. A simple test measures the level.
2. **How often should my newborn feed?** Most newborns feed 8 to 12 times in 24 hours, about every 2 to 3 hours, including at night. Six or more wet nappies a day after the first week and steady weight gain are good signs that feeding is going well.
3. **When are the first vaccines due?** At birth, babies receive BCG, the first oral polio dose and hepatitis B, as per India's national schedule. The next vaccines are due at six weeks. Keep the vaccination card safe and bring it to every visit.

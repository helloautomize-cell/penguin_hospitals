**H1: Irregular periods**

A period is called irregular when the gap between periods keeps changing a lot, is shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or when periods stop for three months or more. Irregular periods are common and often have a simple cause, but they can be a sign of a hormone problem. Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande finds the reason and explains what can help.

### Common causes
- **Puberty and the years before menopause**, when cycles are naturally less regular
- **PCOS**, a common hormone condition
- **Thyroid problems**, both underactive and overactive
- **Stress, illness, sudden weight change or intense exercise**
- **Breastfeeding**
- **Pregnancy**, always worth ruling out
- **Some contraceptives and medicines**
- **High prolactin**, a hormone from the brain

### When to see a doctor
- Periods stop for three months or more, and you are not pregnant
- Cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- Bleeding between periods or after sex
- Very heavy or very painful periods
- Periods have not started by age 15
- You are trying to get pregnant

### What happens at Penguin Hospital
1. **A talk.** About your cycle pattern, weight, stress, medicines and plans for pregnancy. A period-tracking app or diary helps.
2. **Examination and scan.** An ultrasound looks at the womb and ovaries.
3. **Blood tests.** Usually thyroid, prolactin, other hormones and sometimes sugar.
4. **A plan.** Treatment depends on the cause: lifestyle changes, treating the thyroid, hormone treatment to regulate cycles, or fertility help if you want to conceive.

### FAQs
1. **Is it normal for periods to be irregular in teenagers?** Yes, for the first two or three years after periods start. Cycles can range from about 21 to 45 days. See a doctor if periods have not settled after three years, stop for three months, or are very heavy or painful.
2. **Can stress really delay my period?** Yes. Stress affects the part of the brain that controls the hormones of your cycle. Exams, work pressure, illness or a big life change can delay or stop a period. If it happens often, a check-up helps to rule out other causes.
3. **I missed a period but my pregnancy test is negative. What now?** Repeat the test after a week. If still negative and no period comes, see a doctor. Common reasons include stress, weight change, thyroid problems and PCOS. A scan and blood tests usually find the cause.
4. **Will irregular periods affect my chance of getting pregnant?** They can make it harder to know when you ovulate, and some causes, like PCOS, reduce how often you ovulate. Most causes are treatable. If you are trying to conceive and your periods are irregular, see a doctor early instead of waiting a year.

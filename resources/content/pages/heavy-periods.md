**H1: Heavy periods (heavy menstrual bleeding)**

Periods are heavy when they stop you doing your normal day: changing pads every one to two hours, passing large clots, bleeding through clothes at night, or bleeding for more than seven days. Heavy periods can cause tiredness and anaemia, and often have a treatable cause. At Penguin Hospital, Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande finds the cause and explains every option, from medicines to keyhole surgery.

### Signs your periods are too heavy
- Changing a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours
- Clots larger than a one-rupee coin
- Using two pads at once, or waking at night to change
- Bleeding for more than 7 days
- Tiredness, dizziness or breathlessness

### Common causes
- **Fibroids**, non-cancerous growths in the womb
- **Polyps**, small growths in the lining of the womb
- **Adenomyosis**, where the lining grows into the muscle of the womb
- **Hormone imbalance**, common in teenagers and before menopause
- **Thyroid problems**
- **Bleeding disorders**, or blood-thinning medicines
- **Rarely, thickening or cancer of the womb lining**, especially after 45

### What happens at Penguin Hospital
- A talk and examination
- A blood count to check for anaemia, and thyroid tests
- An ultrasound of the womb and ovaries
- Sometimes a hysteroscopy (a thin camera into the womb) or a sample of the lining

### Treatment options
Dr. Lakshmi decides the right treatment for you after examining you. Options include:
- Medicines taken during the period to reduce bleeding
- Hormone treatment, including a hormone-releasing coil placed in the womb
- Iron to treat anaemia
- Hysteroscopic removal of polyps or small fibroids
- Laparoscopic removal of fibroids
- Hysterectomy, removal of the womb, only when other options have not worked and you do not want more children

**When to get help straight away.** Bleeding that soaks a pad every hour for several hours, fainting, chest pain or severe breathlessness. Call 108 or 112, or come to our emergency entrance.

### FAQs
1. **How do I know if my bleeding is really heavy?** If your period affects work, school, sleep or going out, or you feel tired and breathless, it is worth a check. Keep a note of how many pads you use each day and whether you pass clots. That helps Dr. Lakshmi understand your bleeding.
2. **Can heavy periods cause anaemia?** Yes. Losing a lot of blood every month can lower your iron and haemoglobin, causing tiredness, breathlessness, hair fall and pale skin. A simple blood test shows if you are anaemic. Iron treatment helps, but the cause of the bleeding also needs treating.
3. **Will I need a hysterectomy?** Most women do not. Many are helped by medicines, a hormone coil, or removal of a polyp or fibroid. Hysterectomy is considered only when other treatments have not worked and you have completed your family. The decision is always yours.
4. **My periods became heavy after 40. Should I worry?** Changes in bleeding are common before menopause, often because of hormone shifts or fibroids. But new heavy or irregular bleeding after 40 should be checked, sometimes with a sample of the womb lining, to rule out a more serious cause.

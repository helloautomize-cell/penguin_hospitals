**H1: Endometriosis**

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the womb grows outside it, on the ovaries, the tubes, or the lining of the pelvis. It bleeds and becomes inflamed with each cycle, causing pain and sometimes scarring. It is common, often missed for years, and treatable. Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande takes period pain seriously and works with you on a plan for pain and fertility.

### Symptoms
- Period pain that stops you working, studying or sleeping, and that gets worse over time
- Pain during or after sex
- Pain when passing stools or urine, especially during periods
- Long-term pelvic pain
- Difficulty getting pregnant
- Tiredness

### How it is diagnosed
- A detailed talk about your pain
- An examination and ultrasound, which can show endometriomas ("chocolate cysts") and some deep endometriosis
- Laparoscopy, a keyhole look inside the tummy, which is the only way to see all types and can treat them at the same time

### Treatment
- **Pain relief** with medicines during periods
- **Hormone treatment** to reduce or stop periods and calm the endometriosis
- **Laparoscopic surgery** to remove endometriosis patches and cysts, and free up scarring
- **Fertility support** when pregnancy is the goal

Treatment depends on your pain, your age and whether you want to become pregnant soon. Endometriosis can come back, so follow-up matters.

### FAQs
1. **Is it normal for periods to be very painful?** Some cramping is normal. Pain that stops you going to work or school, does not settle with simple pain relief, or gets worse each year is not something to just live with. It can be a sign of endometriosis or adenomyosis, and it deserves a check-up.
2. **Can I get pregnant with endometriosis?** Many women with endometriosis conceive naturally. Some find it harder, because of scarring or effects on the ovaries. Surgery and fertility treatment can help. If you have endometriosis and have been trying for six months without success, see Dr. Lakshmi.
3. **Does endometriosis go away after pregnancy or menopause?** Symptoms often ease in pregnancy and after menopause, when hormone levels change, but pregnancy is not a cure. Dr. Lakshmi will never advise pregnancy as a treatment. Long-term hormone treatment or surgery may be needed to control symptoms.
4. **Will I need surgery?** Not always. Many women manage well with pain relief and hormone treatment. Surgery is advised when medicines do not help, when there are large cysts, or when fertility is affected. Keyhole surgery usually means a short hospital stay.

**H1: Keyhole heart surgery (MICS)**

Minimally invasive cardiac surgery (MICS) means operating on the heart through a small cut, usually 5 to 8 cm, between the ribs on the side of the chest, instead of opening the breastbone. For suitable patients it can mean less pain, less blood loss, a smaller scar and a quicker return to daily life. Dr. Ashish Baviskar performs MICS and will tell you honestly whether it suits you.

### Operations that can be done by MICS
- Mitral valve repair or replacement
- Aortic valve replacement
- Some bypass operations (MICS CABG)
- Closing some holes in the heart in adults (ASD)
- Removing some heart tumours
[CONFIRM: operations Dr. Ashish performs by MICS]

### Who it may suit
It depends on your heart problem, your chest shape, your lungs, your weight and your other conditions. It is not suitable for everyone, and open surgery remains the safer choice for some operations and some patients. Dr. Ashish decides after reviewing your echo, angiography and CT scan.

### Compared with open surgery
| | MICS | Open (sternotomy) |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | 5 to 8 cm on the side of the chest | About 20 to 25 cm down the middle |
| Breastbone | Not cut | Cut and wired |
| Typical hospital stay | Often shorter | About 5 to 7 days |
| Return to driving and lifting | Often within weeks | Usually 6 to 8 weeks |

Both carry the risks of heart surgery, which Dr. Ashish explains before you consent.

### Recovery
- A short stay in the intensive care unit after surgery
- Walking with help the next day
- Breathing exercises to keep the lungs clear
- A gradual return to activity, with cardiac rehabilitation advice

### FAQs
1. **Is keyhole heart surgery less risky than open surgery?** It is not automatically safer. It mainly offers a faster, more comfortable recovery for suitable patients. The main risks of heart surgery are similar either way. The safest approach depends on your heart and health, and Dr. Ashish will recommend the one with the best balance for you.
2. **Will the heart-lung machine be used?** For most valve operations and hole repairs done by MICS, yes, connected through vessels in the groin or chest. Some bypass operations can be done on a beating heart without it. Dr. Ashish will explain what your operation involves.
3. **How soon can I go back to work after MICS?** Many people return to desk work in two to four weeks, earlier than after open surgery, because the breastbone is not cut. Heavy physical work takes longer. Your recovery plan will give you a timeline.
4. **Is MICS more expensive?** Costs depend on the operation, the hospital and the materials used. Our desk will give you an estimate before admission and help with insurance. [Compliance: no prices on the site]

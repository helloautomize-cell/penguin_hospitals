**H1: Heart, lung and vascular surgery in Chinchwad**

Cardiothoracic and vascular surgery treats the heart, the lungs and the blood vessels. At Penguin Hospital it is led by Dr. Ashish Baviskar, a CTVS surgeon with more than 15 years of practice, who also performs keyhole heart surgery (MICS). Many patients who come worried about an operation leave with a clear plan, and often that plan does not involve surgery.

### Heart (glass cards)
- **Heart check-up.** ECG, echo and stress test, with a specialist's review.
- **Keyhole heart surgery (MICS).** Heart surgery through a small cut between the ribs.
- **Bypass surgery (CABG).** New routes for blood around blocked heart arteries.
- **Heart valve surgery.** Repair or replacement of a tight or leaking valve.

### Chest and vessels (glass cards)
- **Lung and chest surgery.** Growths, fluid, air leaks and injuries.
- **Varicose veins.** Swollen, aching leg veins.
- **Leg artery disease.** Leg pain on walking, wounds that do not heal.
- **AV fistula for dialysis.** Lasting access for kidney dialysis.

### FAQs
1. **What is the difference between a cardiologist and a heart surgeon?** A cardiologist diagnoses heart conditions and treats them with medicines and procedures through the blood vessels, such as angiography and stents. A cardiothoracic surgeon operates on the heart, lungs and major vessels, for example bypass or valve surgery. The two often work together to decide what is best.
2. **I have been told I need heart surgery. Can I get a second opinion?** Yes, and it is sensible. Bring your angiography CD, echo report and other reports. Dr. Ashish will review them, explain what they show, and tell you whether surgery, a stent or medicines is the right option, and why.
3. **Is heart surgery done at Penguin Hospital itself?** [CONFIRM: which operations are done in-house and which at a partner hospital] Dr. Ashish will tell you at your first visit where your operation would take place and who would be in the team.
4. **Can women see Dr. Ashish?** Of course. Heart disease is the leading cause of death in Indian women too, and its symptoms in women can be less typical: tiredness, breathlessness, or pain in the back, jaw or stomach. Dr. Ashish sees men and women of all ages.

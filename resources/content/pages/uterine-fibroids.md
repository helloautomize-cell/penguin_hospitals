**H1: Uterine fibroids**

Fibroids are non-cancerous growths of muscle in the womb (uterus). They are very common, especially between 30 and 50, and many cause no problems at all. When they cause heavy periods, pain or pressure, or affect fertility, Dr. Lakshmi Kalbande explains the options, from watching and waiting to keyhole removal.

### Symptoms
- Heavy or long periods
- Pain or pressure in the lower tummy or back
- Needing to pass urine often, or constipation
- A swollen tummy
- Pain during sex
- Difficulty getting pregnant, or miscarriage, depending on where the fibroid is

### Types (by where they grow)
- **Inside the lining (submucosal):** most likely to cause heavy bleeding and fertility problems
- **In the wall (intramural):** the most common type
- **On the outside (subserosal):** may press on the bladder or bowel

### What happens at Penguin Hospital
An ultrasound shows the number, size and position of fibroids, often on the day of your visit. A blood count checks for anaemia.

### Treatment options
- **Watch and wait.** Small fibroids without symptoms usually need only a scan every year or so.
- **Medicines** to reduce bleeding and pain, or to shrink fibroids for a time before surgery.
- **Hysteroscopic removal** of fibroids inside the lining, through the vagina, with no cuts on the tummy.
- **Laparoscopic myomectomy:** keyhole removal of fibroids, keeping the womb. Suitable for many women who want children.
- **Open myomectomy** for very large or many fibroids.
- **Hysterectomy** when fibroids keep causing problems and you do not want more children.

### FAQs
1. **Can fibroids turn into cancer?** Very rarely. Fibroids are benign. A rare cancer of the womb muscle exists, but it is not thought to develop from ordinary fibroids. A fibroid that grows quickly after menopause needs closer checks, and Dr. Lakshmi will advise you if anything on your scan needs further tests.
2. **Can I get pregnant with fibroids?** Many women with fibroids have healthy pregnancies. Fibroids inside the lining of the womb can make it harder to conceive or carry a pregnancy, and removing them may help. Fibroids can grow in pregnancy and cause pain. Dr. Lakshmi will advise whether yours need treatment first.
3. **Do fibroids go away on their own?** They do not usually disappear, but they often shrink after menopause, when hormone levels fall. If yours are small and cause no symptoms, regular scans may be all you need.
4. **How long is recovery after laparoscopic fibroid removal?** Most women go home in one to two days and return to light work in about two weeks. Avoid heavy lifting for four to six weeks. If you plan a pregnancy, Dr. Lakshmi will advise how long to wait, often three to six months.

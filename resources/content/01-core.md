**H1: Emergency care, day and night**

Our emergency team is in the hospital around the clock, including for women in labour and for pregnancy emergencies. In a life-threatening emergency, call 108 or 112 for an ambulance first, then call us on +91 95189 04156 so we are ready when you arrive.

Big buttons: Call 108 · Call 112 · Call Penguin Hospital

### Come straight in, or call 108, if you notice:
**In pregnancy**
- Bleeding from the vagina, at any stage
- Water breaking, or a gush or steady leak of fluid
- Regular, painful contractions before 37 weeks
- Your baby moving less than usual, or not at all
- A bad headache, blurred vision, or sudden swelling of the face and hands
- Fits (seizures), fainting or severe tummy pain

**After birth**
- Heavy bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour, or large clots
- Fever, or a wound that is red, hot or leaking
- Pain or swelling in one leg, or breathlessness

**Heart and chest**
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, especially with sweating, sickness, or pain in the arm, jaw or back
- Sudden breathlessness
- Fainting, or a very fast or irregular heartbeat
- A cold, pale, painful leg

**Anyone**
- Signs of stroke: face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech
- Serious injury, heavy bleeding, or someone who cannot be woken

### On the way
- Do not drive yourself if you have chest pain or are in strong labour.
- Bring your reports, pregnancy file and medicines, but do not delay to find them.
- If the person stops breathing, call 108 and start chest compressions if you know how.

### What we can and cannot do
We handle emergencies in pregnancy and birth, and heart, chest and vascular emergencies within our services. When a patient needs care we do not provide, such as major trauma [CONFIRM], we stabilise them and arrange transfer to the right hospital.

### FAQs
1. **Do I need to call before coming in labour?** It helps. Call +91 95189 04156 on the way so the labour team is ready. But never delay coming in to get through on the phone. If labour is fast or there is bleeding, come straight in or call 108.
2. **Is a doctor available at night?** Yes. A doctor is on duty at night, and the specialists are called in when you need them. [CONFIRM on-duty doctor arrangement]
3. **What if I think I am having a heart attack?** Call 108 or 112 for an ambulance straight away, then call us. Sit or lie down, loosen tight clothing and stay calm. Do not drive yourself, and do not wait to see if the pain passes. Take only the emergency medicine your doctor has already told you to take.
4. **Do you have an ambulance?** [CONFIRM] For emergencies, 108 is the free ambulance service in Maharashtra.

### Stage by stage (text version of the dial, for search and screen readers)
**Weeks 4 to 7. Baby is about the size of a pomegranate seed.** At Penguin: confirm the pregnancy, first visit, folic acid. You may notice: tiredness, nausea for some, sore breasts.
**Weeks 8 to 11. About the size of a jamun.** At Penguin: booking visit with blood tests, dating scan. You may notice: nausea may peak, needing to pass urine more often.
**Weeks 12 to 14. About the size of a lemon.** At Penguin: NT scan and first-trimester screening, a talk about diet, work and travel. You may notice: energy often returns, nausea often eases.
**Weeks 15 to 19. About the size of a guava.** At Penguin: monthly check of weight, blood pressure and urine, blood tests if due. You may notice: a small bump, first flutters for some.
**Weeks 20 to 23. About the size of a mango.** At Penguin: anomaly scan, usually between 18 and 22 weeks, iron and calcium checked. You may notice: clear baby movements, back ache for some.
**Weeks 24 to 28. About the size of a papaya.** At Penguin: sugar test for diabetes in pregnancy, blood pressure watched closely. You may notice: heartburn, leg cramps at night.
**Weeks 29 to 34. About the size of a coconut.** At Penguin: visits every two weeks, growth scan if needed, birth plan talk. You may notice: breathlessness on stairs, practice tightenings.
**Weeks 35 to 40. About the size of a small watermelon.** At Penguin: weekly visits, baby's position checked, signs of labour explained. You may notice: pressure low down, poor sleep, nesting.

Fine print: A general guide for healthy pregnancies. Your own schedule may differ. Sex determination of the foetus is a crime under the PCPNDT Act; we do not disclose the sex of the baby.

### FAQs
1. **How accurate is a due date calculator?** It gives an estimate based on a 28-day cycle. Only about one baby in twenty is born on the due date itself; most arrive in the two weeks either side. If your cycles are longer, shorter or irregular, the dating scan between 8 and 13 weeks gives a more reliable date.
2. **When should I have my first visit?** Book as soon as your home test is positive, ideally around 6 to 8 weeks. Come sooner if you have bleeding, strong one-sided pain, a past ectopic pregnancy, diabetes, high blood pressure or thyroid problems, or if you take regular medicines.
3. **How many scans will I have?** Most healthy pregnancies have three or four: a dating scan, the NT scan around 11 to 14 weeks, the anomaly scan around 18 to 22 weeks, and sometimes a growth scan later. Dr. Lakshmi may advise more if your pregnancy needs closer watching.
4. **When will I feel my baby move?** Most first-time mothers notice flutters between 18 and 22 weeks, and earlier in a second pregnancy. After about 28 weeks, learn your baby's usual pattern. If movements slow down or stop, come in the same day.
5. **Is the week counted from conception?** No. Pregnancy weeks are counted from the first day of your last period, about two weeks before conception. So at "4 weeks pregnant", the baby has been growing for about two weeks.

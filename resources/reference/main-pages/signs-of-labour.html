Pregnancy

Signs of labour, and when to come to hospital

Dr. Lakshmi Vijay KalbandeObstetrician and gynaecologistPublished CONFIRM launch date · 6 min read
In brief

Labour usually starts with regular, strengthening contractions, a "show" or your water breaking. For a first baby, come in when contractions are strong and about 5 minutes apart for an hour. Come straight away if your water breaks, you see bleeding, or your baby is moving less.

Most women worry about two things in the last weeks of pregnancy: will I know when labour has started, and will I come in too early or too late? Here is how to tell.

False labour or real labour?

In the last weeks, many women feel practice tightenings (Braxton Hicks contractions). They are usually irregular, do not get stronger, and often settle when you rest, walk or change position. Real labour contractions come at regular intervals, get closer together, last longer, and become stronger whatever you do.

The early signs

  • A show: a small amount of sticky, pink or brownish mucus from the vagina. Labour may start in hours or days.
  • Backache or period-like cramps that come and go.
  • Loose stools or an unsettled stomach.
  • Your water breaking: a gush or a steady trickle of clear or pinkish fluid.

When to come in

  • First baby: when contractions are strong, regular and about 5 minutes apart, each lasting about a minute, for at least an hour.
  • Second or later baby: sooner, as labour is often faster. Many women come when contractions are about 10 minutes apart.
  • Always straight away if your water breaks, if the fluid is green or brown, if there is bleeding, if your baby is moving less, if you have a bad headache or blurred vision, or if pains start before 37 weeks.

If you live far, in Wakad, Hinjawadi or Moshi, leave a little earlier, especially at peak traffic times.

What to bring

Your pregnancy file and reports, ID and insurance card, comfortable clothes, slippers, toiletries, maternity pads, a few sets of baby clothes, caps and socks, and a phone charger. Pack the bag by 36 weeks.

Call us on the way. Call +91 95189 04156 so the labour team knows you are coming. If labour is very fast, there is heavy bleeding, or you have a fit, call 108.

Questions about labour

Ask on WhatsApp
What if my water breaks but I have no pains?
Come in. Once the waters break, the baby is less protected from infection, and we need to check you and your baby. Note the time and the colour of the fluid.
Should I eat in early labour?
Light snacks and fluids are usually fine in early labour at home. Once you are in hospital, the team will advise you, especially if an epidural or caesarean is possible.
Can I have an epidural if I come in late?
An epidural takes time to set up, so ask early. If labour is very far along, there may not be time. Discuss your wishes at your antenatal visits.
